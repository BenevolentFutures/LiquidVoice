02 / Listen

You talk to your computer all day now. Prompts for agents, replies, notes.

Dictation should be the fastest way in, but it fails quietly. The text lands in the wrong window. A terminal swallows it. The clipboard you were holding is gone. You find out when you look up.

The problem is not hearing you. The problem is delivery.

MouthKeys is on-device dictation for macOS. Hold or tap a hotkey, speak, and the text lands where you were typing when you stopped. The speech model runs on your Mac: Parakeet, Nemotron, Cohere Transcribe, Whisper or Apple Speech. When a paste fails, it tells you and puts the words on your clipboard.

03 / What you get

10 items
01

Paste you can trust

When a paste fails in a way the app can detect, a card shows and the text goes on your clipboard. Your own clipboard comes back, images and files included. Back-to-back dictations queue instead of dropping.

02

A faster stop

Stop-path work outside the model fell from a 145 ms median to 4 ms. A stalled model no longer loses the recording: it is kept for Reprocess, even across a restart.

03

Built for terminals

Ghostty and c11 always get Reliable Paste, so agents in a terminal get every word.

04

Spoken Send

End with "send it" and Return follows the text, in c11, only in the pane you stopped in. Off by default.

05

A live voice trace

A scrolling trace of your voice, calibrated to the recording, so you can see it hearing you.

06

Live counters

A timer, a word count and words per minute while you speak.

07

Your mic at a glance

The active mic shows in the overlay, with the battery level of a Hollyland lapel mic.

08

A history browser

Recent dictations, each one click from copy or reprocess. Drag the overlay anywhere.

09

An interface that stays quiet

Square, flat, monochrome, with orange marking only what is live. Nothing on screen moves unless it means something.

10

Hotkeys that behave

Holds that always end, removed mics that stay removed, media resumed only if we paused it, and a hotkey to reprocess the last dictation.

04 / Install

Quick setup
01

Download the DMG

Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so it opens like any other app.

Download
02

Drag MouthKeys to Applications

Open the DMG and drag the app across. It does not update itself: to update, download the new release and replace the app. Your settings, dictionary and history stay.

Next
03

Grant Microphone and Accessibility

Microphone, to hear you. Accessibility, to type into other apps. Setup walks you through both.

Next
04

Pick a speech model and talk

The default on Apple Silicon, Parakeet TDT v3, is about a 460 MiB download. It runs on your Mac from then on.

Talk
System

macOS 15 or later. Apple Speech needs macOS 26.

Chip

Parakeet, Nemotron and Cohere, the defaults, need Apple Silicon. Whisper up to Medium also runs on Intel, untested.

Cost

Free. No account, no subscription, no trial.

Build it yourself. Building needs Xcode 26. ./build.sh install builds a signed Release, backs up the installed app and swaps the new one in, printing the command to roll back. Details in the README.

$ git clone --depth 1 -b liquid-voice \
    https://github.com/BenevolentFutures/MouthKeys.git
$ cd MouthKeys
$ ./build.sh install

05 / Privacy

TelemetryNone

Analytics are hard-wired off and the keys are blank.

AccountNone

Nothing to sign up for.

Your voiceLocal

Audio and text stay on your Mac unless you choose otherwise.

UpdaterOff

The app never phones home to update itself.

The app goes online for three things, each your choice:

  • downloading the speech model you pick;
  • AI cleanup, if you set up a provider (off, untested and unsupported);
  • Apple ASR Legacy, which lets macOS choose where speech is recognized, possibly Apple's servers. Every other engine runs on the Mac.

06 / Built on FluidVoice

MouthKeys is a fork of FluidVoice by altic-dev. FluidVoice did the hard part: the speech pipeline, the model integrations, hotkeys, typing into other apps, settings and model downloads. The models come from NVIDIA, Cohere, OpenAI and Apple.

We dictate into coding agents all day, and we needed one thing done perfectly: every word lands where we were typing. So we cut MouthKeys down to that job. If you want FluidVoice's AI features, FluidVoice is the app for that.
